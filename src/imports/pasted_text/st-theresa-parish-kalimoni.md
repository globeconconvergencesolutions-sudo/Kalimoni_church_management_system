Our History
Kalimoni Parish history spans from 1912, when it was started by the Holy Ghost Fathers. It eventually became a parish in 1927. Over the years, the parish has grown and given birth to many other parishes which currently form the Ruiru and Thika Deaneries.
Going by the history of the growth of the church in Kenya, Kalimoni Catholic Church was established around 10 years after the missionaries moved inland and set the first church in Nairobi (St. Austin).
In 2000, the Vincentian Congregation took up the responsibility of the parish from the Holy Ghost Fathers. The pioneering service of the first Parish Priest, Late Rev. Fr. James Edavazhira VC, laid a firm foundation for the Vincentian mission in Kalimoni. Since then, through the dedication and pastoral commitment of many Vincentian Fathers, the parish has grown steadily in faith, unity, and service. 
Since 2000, the Vincentian Congregation priests have been carrying out their ministry in the parish. There has been tremendous growth in faith. This is characterised by the number of new parishes created since 2000. The parishes are:
1.	St. Augustine Parish, JKUAT Chaplaincy
2.	Presentation of the Lord Parish, Juja Farm
3.	Divine Mercy Parish, Kenyatta Road
There has also been an increase in the number of outstations in all these parishes, with St. Theresa Kalimoni having added two outstations after Divine Mercy was elevated to a parish.
Our Growth Journey
The parish is rooted in the tradition of Small Christian Communities (SCC), commonly known as “Jumuiyas” in Swahili, where faith is lived in homes and neighbourhoods. These small groups have become the heartbeat of the parish, and over the years they have experienced remarkable growth.
•	2002: 15 Jumuiyas in the parish
•	2008: Jumuiyas grew to 25 Jumuiyas
•	2016: The number rose to 30 Jumuiyas
•	2019: Growth reached 48 Jumuiyas
•	2022: The number surged to 72 Jumuiyas
In 2024, two outstations were established;
1.	St. Dominic Outstation – (August 2024)
2.	Holy Archangels Outstation – (September 2024)
In December 2024, a new Grotto was consecrated as a project of the Catholic Men Association (CMA). The Grotto soon became a centre for Marian devotion and personal prayer.
In February 2025, the Divine Mercy Chapel was consecrated as the fruit of the efforts of the Catholic Women Association (CWA), adding a new spiritual landmark to the parish’s grounds.
On Good Friday of 2025, the Parish Way of the Cross concluded at the foot of a beautiful crucifix erected where the former grotto once stood. This inspiring project was undertaken by the Youth Serving Christ (YSC) and Young Catholic Adults (YCA), highlighting the active role of youth in parish life.

About the Vincentian Congregation
The Vincentian Congregation is a clerical society within the Catholic Church, inspired by the spirituality and works of St. Vincent de Paul, who is honoured as its patron.
Guided by the motto “He has sent me to proclaim the good news to the poor” (Luke 4:18), the congregation was established in India in 1904 by Rev. Fr. Varkey Kattarath.
On February 11, 1968, it was elevated to the status of a society of pontifical right, marking its recognition at the universal level of the Church.
The Vincentians in Kenya
The Vincentian Missionaries arrived in Kenya in the late 1990s. True to the spirit of St. Vincent, they dedicated themselves to pastoral care, retreats, missions, and social outreach.
The Vincentians in Kalimoni
In 1999, the Vincentian Congregation came to St. Theresa Parish, Kalimoni, where they served under the then parish priest, Fr. Gogan H.G.F. In 2000, when Fr. Gogan left for his holiday and unfortunately fell ill, making it impossible for him to return to Kenya, the Bishop entrusted the parish to the Vincentian Congregation.
Under the leadership of Fr. James Edavazhira, V.C., the Vincentians officially took charge, bringing with them a renewed missionary zeal that has shaped the spiritual, pastoral, and social life of the parish.
Since then, the Vincentian Fathers have faithfully walked with the Christian community of St. Theresa Parish, Kalimoni, embodying the spirit of St. Vincent de Paul: service to God through service to humanity. Their mission here has been marked by evangelisation, joyful celebrations, works of charity, and a strong spirit of leadership and unity.

The Sisters of the Handmaids of the Holy Child Jesus (HHCJ)
History and Presence in Kalimoni
Having already spread to other Dioceses within Kenya, the Sisters of the Handmaids of the Holy Child Jesus (HHCJ) longed to serve in the Archdiocese of Nairobi. After prayerful reflection and consultation, in 2000, the sisters presented their request to the then Archbishop of Nairobi, Rt. Rev. Ndingi Mwana ‘Nzeki, who graciously permitted them to take over a dispensary and a girls’ boarding facility previously managed by the Missionary Sisters of the Precious Blood.
Thus, on February 4, 2000, a new HHCJ community was born in the Archdiocese of Nairobi with Sr. Evangeline Gitonga and Sr. Theresa Barnie as the first community members. Later that same year, on December 3, 2000, the community grew with the arrival of Sr. Florence Onuzuruike and Sr. Justina Umeh.
As the mission expanded, more sisters joined the community. Among them was Sr. Juliana Mose, who came in 2006 and has since faithfully served as the administrator of the dispensary. Under their dedicated service, the once modest dispensary has grown into Kalimoni Mission Hospital, now a fully-fledged level four hospital. It serves the wider community.
The former girls’ boarding facility has been transformed into a home for street boys, offering rehabilitation and supporting their reintegration into family and society.
Their Apostolate
1.	Health Ministry – through Kalimoni Mission Hospital.
2.	Education Ministry – through Kalimoni Comprehensive School
3.	Pastoral Ministry – by guiding Small Christian Communities (SCC), ministering to children and women, and supporting parish liturgy.
4.	Charitable Works – by standing with the poor, vulnerable, and marginalised, offering practical assistance and encouragement.
Impact
